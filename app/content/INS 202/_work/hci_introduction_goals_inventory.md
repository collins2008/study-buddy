## 1. HCI Foundations & Definitions
- **HCI (Human-Computer Interaction)**: A cross-disciplinary field concerned with the design, evaluation, and implementation of interactive computing systems for human use and with the study of major phenomena surrounding them (ACM SIGCHI formal definition).
- **Interdisciplinary Pillars of HCI**:
  1. *Computer Science*: Technical software and hardware realization of interactive systems.
  2. *Cognitive Psychology*: Understanding human perception, mental models, attention, memory limits, and decision-making.
  3. *Ergonomics / Human Factors*: Physical capabilities, comfort, biomechanics, visual fatigue, workstation posture.
  4. *Graphic & Visual Design*: Typography, layout, visual hierarchy, aesthetic appeal.
  5. *Linguistics*: Language syntax, error messaging terminology, natural language interfaces.
- **The Historic Cost Shift**:
  - In 1953 (IBM 701, ~33 kb memory), computer hardware was so astronomically expensive that a single machine's cost equaled the annual salaries of 200 human programmers. Humans were expected to spend laborious days preparing punch cards to save scarce machine CPU seconds.
  - Today, computing hardware is cheap and ubiquitous, while human time, attention, and cognitive effort are the scarce, expensive resources. Modern systems must be engineered around human cognitive convenience.
- **Business & Organizational Benefits of Usable Systems**:
  - *Gaining a Competitive Edge*: Higher *Conversion Rate* (the percentage of visitors taking a desired action, such as purchasing).
  - *Reduced Development & Maintenance Costs*: Eliminates designing features that users neither want nor use.
  - *Increased Productivity*: Users complete workflows faster with fewer fatal errors.
  - *Lower Customer Support Costs*: Technical support telephone calls cost vendors between $12 and $250 per call; intuitive self-explanatory interfaces dramatically reduce support call volume.

## 2. The Five Foundational Goals of HCI (MANDATORY EXAM TOPIC)
*Explicitly flagged in lecturer notebook: "list the goals of HCI => IMPORTANT FOR EXAM!!!"*
1. **Safety**:
   - Protecting human users from physical injury, catastrophic accidents, or fatality (critical in life-or-death systems: air traffic control, nuclear power plant monitors, hospital intensive care telemetry, fly-by-wire avionics).
   - Protecting critical user data and financial assets from inadvertent, unrecoverable destruction or corruption.
2. **Utility**:
   - The functional capability, services, and features provided by a system.
   - Refers to *what* actions and operations the system allows the user to perform (e.g. searching, calculating, booking flights, printing reports).
3. **Effectiveness**:
   - The degree to which a system allows users to successfully achieve their intended goals completely and accurately.
   - *Utility vs Effectiveness Distinction*: Utility is whether the service exists; Effectiveness is whether users can actually accomplish their goal using that service. (e.g. A bookstore site has the utility to sell books, but if users cannot locate books or check out due to confusing navigation, the site completely lacks effectiveness).
4. **Efficiency**:
   - A measure of the resources (time, keystrokes, clicks, physical effort) expended by a user to accomplish a specific goal once they know how to use the system.
   - High efficiency means users can finish tasks rapidly with minimal wasted motion.
5. **Usability**:
   - A multi-dimensional metric assessing how easy and pleasant a system is to use:
     - *Learnability*: How easily can a first-time user learn the interface?
     - *Memorability*: How easily can a returning user re-establish proficiency after a period of non-use?
     - *Error Handling*: Low error frequency, low error severity, and clear recovery paths.
     - *Satisfaction / Appeal*: How well users like the system both immediately and over extended use.
