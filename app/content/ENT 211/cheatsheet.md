### 1. Foundational Concepts & Distinctions
- **Definition of Entrepreneurship**: The process of identifying opportunities, assembling and coordinating scarce resources, taking calculated risks, testing assumptions, and creating value through business activities.
- **The 5-Step Value Creation Loop**:
  $$\text{Spot (Opportunity)} \longrightarrow \text{Mobilise (Resources)} \longrightarrow \text{Test (Assumptions)} \longrightarrow \text{Act (Calculated Risk)} \longrightarrow \text{Create Value}$$
- **Entrepreneur vs. Intrapreneur**:
  - *Entrepreneur*: Independent founder; bears full personal financial liability; total strategic autonomy; captures 100% equity upside.
  - *Intrapreneur*: Innovates *inside* an established corporate firm; shielded personal financial risk (corporate salary); backed by corporate balance sheets and licenses; constrained by corporate bureaucracy and lacks equity ownership.
- **Entrepreneur vs. Traditional Manager (Slide 8)**:
  - *Strategic Focus*: Opportunity-driven (entrepreneur) vs. Resource-preservation driven (manager).
  - *Risk Posture*: Embraces calculated risk under uncertainty vs. Controls and minimizes operational variance.
  - *Attitude to Change*: Revolutionary disruption vs. Enforcing stability, rules, and standardized operating procedures (SOPs).
- **Invention vs. Innovation (Slide 10)**:
  - *Invention*: Creating or discovering a novel idea, formula, or prototype (e.g., lab prototype of a solar water purifier).
  - *Innovation*: Implementing and commercializing an idea in a way that creates practical customer and business value (e.g., mass-producing and distributing the purifier).
  - *Rule*: Invention turns cash into knowledge; Innovation turns knowledge back into cash and value.
- **Business Idea vs. Business Opportunity (Slide 4)**:
  - *Business Idea*: An unproven mental notion ("I want to open a gym").
  - *Business Opportunity*: An attractive, timely, viable prospect supported by empirical evidence of real customer pain, accessible markets, and sustainable economics.

### 2. Forms & Sources of Innovation
- **Five Types of Innovation (Slide 11)**:
  1. *Product*: New or improved physical good (e.g., electric tricycle with swappable batteries).
  2. *Process*: Better, faster, or cheaper way to do work (e.g., digital GPS fleet tracking replacing paper manifests).
  3. *Service*: Superior customer experience and delivery (e.g., automated appointment SMS reminders).
  4. *Marketing*: Novel packaging, pricing, placement, or promotion (e.g., 50-Naira micro-sachets for daily wage earners).
  5. *Business Model*: Restructuring value creation and capture (e.g., ColdHubs "Pay-As-You-Store" daily refrigeration subscription).
- **Degrees of Novelty**:
  - *Incremental*: Small-scale continuous refinements (accounts for $\sim 85\%$ of market innovations).
  - *Radical / Breakthrough*: Entirely novel technological architecture creating brand-new industries.
  - *Disruptive Innovation (Clayton Christensen)*: Simpler, cheaper solution entering at the low end or non-consuming market; incumbents ignore it; entrant improves and migrates upmarket, displacing established firms (e.g., POS agency banking disrupting bank branches).
- **Peter Drucker's 7 Sources of Innovation**:
  1. The Unexpected (successes or failures), 2. Incongruities (gap between reality and expectation), 3. Process Need (operational bottlenecks), 4. Industry/Market Shifts, 5. Demographics (Nigeria's youth bulge), 6. Changes in Perception, 7. New Knowledge.

### 3. Theories of Entrepreneurship (EXAM CORE)
- **Economic Theories**:
  - *Joseph A. Schumpeter*: **Five New Combinations** (new good, new production method, new market, new supply source, new industry organization). **Creative Destruction**: continuous industrial mutation where dynamic new firms destroy obsolete, inefficient legacy incumbents.
  - *Frank H. Knight (1921)*: **Risk vs. Uncertainty**. Risk has calculable mathematical probabilities and is *insurable/plannable*; Uncertainty has unmeasurable odds and is *non-insurable*. Profit is the reward for bearing uninsurable uncertainty.
  - *Israel Kirzner*: **Entrepreneurial Alertness & Arbitrage**. Spontaneously noticing price discrepancies and unmet needs without search costs, buying low and selling high to steer the market toward equilibrium.
  - *Harvey Leibenstein*: **X-Inefficiency & Gap-Filling**. Organizations suffer from internal slack and waste; the entrepreneur acts as an *input-completer* and *gap-filler* to restore efficiency.
- **Psychological Theory**:
  - *David McClelland*: **Need for Achievement ($n\text{-Ach}$)**. Entrepreneurs possess an intense drive to set challenging but attainable goals, take personal responsibility, and demand immediate, concrete feedback.
- **Sociological Theories**:
  - *Max Weber*: **Protestant Ethic & Spirit of Capitalism**. Religious/cultural values of hard work as a calling, frugality, and systematic profit reinvestment rather than hedonistic personal luxury.
  - *Everett Hagen*: **Status Withdrawal**. When a social group suffers historical loss of status or prestige, its members channel creative frustrations into independent commercial enterprise.
- **Action & Resource Frameworks**:
  - *Resource-Based View (RBV)*: Sustainable competitive advantage requires **VRIO** resources (Valuable, Rare, Inimitable, Organized).
  - *Saras Sarasvathy: Effectuation (5 Principles)*:
    1. **Bird in Hand**: Start with available means (Who I am, What I know, Whom I know).
    2. **Affordable Loss**: Risk only what you can personally afford to lose (not expected return).
    3. **Crazy Quilt**: Form self-selecting stakeholder partnerships.
    4. **Lemonade**: Treat unexpected surprises as new opportunities.
    5. **Pilot in the Plane**: Focus on controllable actions rather than predicting the future.

### 4. Opportunity Identification, PESTEL & Feasibility Assessment
- **PESTEL Environmental Scanning (Slide 12)**:
  - **P**olitical: Policy stability, tariffs, subsidies, regional security.
  - **E**conomic: Inflation, interest rates (e.g. 28%), FX volatility, disposable income.
  - **S**ocial: Youth demographics, urbanization, cultural lifestyles.
  - **T**echnological: Mobile penetration, USSD, internet connectivity.
  - **E**nvironmental: Tropical climate, solar irradiance, power grid reliability.
  - **L**egal: CAC incorporation, NAFDAC/SON compliance, FIRS taxation, labor laws.
- **Market Gaps (Slide 17)**:
  - *Price Gap* (options too expensive), *Quality Gap*, *Accessibility Gap* (distance/hours), *Product/Service Gap*, *Customer-Segment Gap* (overlooked groups).
- **The Six Pillars of Preliminary Feasibility (Slide 25)**:
  1. *Market*: Sufficient demand, target profile, customer willingness to pay.
  2. *Technical*: Machinery, skilled labor, power/water/internet availability.
  3. *Financial*: CapEx, OpEx, unit contribution margin, cash runway, breakeven volume.
  4. *Operational*: Day-to-day workflow, supply chain, delivery, customer support.
  5. *Legal / Regulatory*: CAC registration, permits, sector compliance.
  6. *Management*: Team competence, domain expertise, resilience.
- **Four Decision Outcomes**: **Proceed**, **Modify / Pivot**, **Research Further**, or **Stop / Kill**.

### 5. Nigerian Industrial & Tech Exemplars
- *Aliko Dangote*: Champion of **backward integration**; transformed Nigeria from bulk importer to local manufacturer and exporter in cement, sugar, and petroleum refining.
- *Tony Elumelu*: Pioneer of **Africapitalism**; private sector driving long-term socioeconomic growth; Tony Elumelu Foundation (TEF) empowering African startup founders.
- *Ibukun Awosika*: Sokoa Chair Centre; exemplary female entrepreneurship in local manufacturing and corporate governance.
- *Fintech Disrupters (Paystack, Flutterwave, Moniepoint, OPay)*: Solved payment friction and unbanked access through POS agency banking and digital rails.
