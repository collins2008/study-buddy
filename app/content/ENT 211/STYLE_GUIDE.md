# ENT 211: Entrepreneurship and Innovation — Course Style Guide
*Nile University of Nigeria | Department of Business Administration / Faculty of Management Sciences*
*Curriculum Standard: NUC CCMAS (Core Curriculum and Minimum Academic Standards)*

---

### 1. Course Nature & Examination Profile
- **Discipline**: Applied Business Administration, Entrepreneurship Theory & Strategic Innovation.
- **Academic Context**: Nile University of Nigeria 200-level general university requirement (CCMAS-aligned).
- **Exam Style**:
  - Theory and applied case scenarios.
  - Distinguishes clearly between conceptual definitions (e.g., Invention vs Innovation, Entrepreneur vs Manager, Business Idea vs Business Opportunity).
  - Heavy emphasis on classical economic and behavioral theories (Schumpeter, Knight, Kirzner, McClelland, Weber, Sarasvathy).
  - Practical application to the **Nigerian business and macroeconomic environment** (Aliko Dangote, Tony Elumelu, Ibukun Awosika, Flutterwave, Paystack, campus laundry/food delivery startups, CBN/BOI financing, infrastructure deficits, currency volatility).
- **Command Words**:
  - *Define*: Concise, authoritative formulation citing key scholars where applicable (e.g., Schumpeter's "new combinations", Drucker's "search for change").
  - *Distinguish / Compare*: Structured contrast matrices (e.g., Risk vs Uncertainty, Idea vs Opportunity, Causation vs Effectuation).
  - *Explain / Analyze*: Detailed explanation of mechanisms (e.g., Creative Destruction, X-inefficiency, PESTEL dimensions).
  - *Apply / Propose*: Practical business problem solving under Nigerian operational constraints.

### 2. Mandatory Core Thinkers & Theoretical Frameworks
1. **Joseph A. Schumpeter**:
   - Innovation as the driving engine of capitalism.
   - Five "New Combinations": (1) New good/quality, (2) New production method, (3) New market, (4) New source of supply, (5) New industrial organization.
   - **Creative Destruction**: Process whereby industrial mutation continuously revolutionizes the economic structure from within, destroying old technologies and firms to create new ones (e.g., digital mobile banking replacing manual bank branches).
2. **Frank H. Knight**:
   - Fundamental distinction between **Risk** (measurable probabilities, calculable, insurable) and **Uncertainty** (true unmeasurable ambiguity, non-insurable).
   - Profit is the entrepreneur's reward for bearing uninsurable uncertainty.
3. **Israel Kirzner**:
   - "Entrepreneurial Alertness" to market disequilibria.
   - Arbitrageur who notices price discrepancies and unmet demand without needing new inventions.
4. **Harvey Leibenstein**:
   - **X-Inefficiency** and input-completing / gap-filling role of the entrepreneur.
5. **David McClelland**:
   - Psychological theory of human motivation: **Need for Achievement ($n\text{-Ach}$)** as the primary driver of entrepreneurial behavior, accompanied by moderate risk-taking and internal locus of control.
6. **Max Weber**:
   - Sociological theory: The Protestant Ethic and the Spirit of Capitalism (religious and cultural values shaping work ethic and capital accumulation).
7. **Saras Sarasvathy**:
   - **Effectuation** (starting with existing means: Who I am, What I know, Whom I know; affordable loss; lemonade principle; crazy quilt of partnerships) vs **Causation** (setting predefined goals and seeking resources to reach them).

### 3. Distinctive Course Concepts & Distinctions
- **Invention vs Innovation**:
  - *Invention*: The creation or discovery of a novel concept, formula, or prototype (e.g., developing a low-cost medical device).
  - *Innovation*: The commercial or practical implementation of an idea that creates tangible customer or societal value (e.g., mass-producing and distributing the device reliably in clinics).
- **Business Idea vs Business Opportunity**:
  - *Business Idea*: An unvalidated thought or concept ("I want to start a drone delivery company").
  - *Business Opportunity*: An attractive, durable, timely idea anchored in validated customer demand, economic feasibility, and competitive advantage.
- **Entrepreneur vs Manager**:
  - *Entrepreneur*: Opportunity-driven, accepts calculated risk, initiates radical change, builds new value.
  - *Manager*: Resource-driven, controls operational risk, prioritizes predictability, optimizes existing systems.
- **PESTEL Framework**:
  - Political, Economic, Social, Technological, Environmental, Legal analysis applied to the Nigerian business ecosystem.

### 4. Question Architecture & Quality Standards
- Every question must follow the strict JSON schema.
- Minimum 30% Level 3–4 (Apply/Analyze) cognitive questions.
- Distractors must represent plausible student misconceptions (e.g., confusing risk with uncertainty, confusing an invention with an innovation, assuming all ideas are opportunities).
- Detailed `why_wrong` explanations on every incorrect option.
- Authentic Nigerian context integration across all topics and mock exams.
