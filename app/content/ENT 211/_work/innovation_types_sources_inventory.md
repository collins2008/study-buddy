# ENT 211 — Topic 2: Innovation vs Invention: Types, Sources & Strategy
## Exhaustive Knowledge Inventory

---

### 1. Conceptualization: What is Innovation? (Slide 9 & 10)
- **Core Definition**: Innovation is the successful implementation and commercialization of new ideas that create measurable value for customers, organizations, or society.
- **The Triad**: **Idea** (a new conceptual possibility) $\to$ **Implementation** (putting the concept into operational practice) $\to$ **Value** (delivering a useful, sustainable, captured outcome).
- **Ubiquity**: Innovation is not restricted to high-tech software labs; it occurs across agriculture, retail, healthcare, logistics, manufacturing, and public governance.

---

### 2. The Critical Distinction: Invention versus Innovation (Slide 10)
- **Invention**:
  - The discovery, design, or creation of a novel scientific concept, mechanism, chemical formulation, or physical prototype that did not previously exist.
  - Exemplar: An engineer in a laboratory inventing a new low-cost solar-powered water filtration membrane.
  - Commercial Reality: An invention may languish in a patent office or university drawer for decades and never be commercialized, adopted, or purchased.
- **Innovation**:
  - The practical, organizational, and commercial realization of an idea (which may or may not be based on a new invention) that achieves widespread market adoption and creates tangible economic utility.
  - Exemplar: Mass-manufacturing the water filter, distributing it through village cooperatives across Northern Nigeria at an affordable price, and training local technicians to service it.
  - Synthesis: *Invention turns cash into knowledge; Innovation turns knowledge back into cash and value.*

---

### 3. The Five Types/Forms of Innovation (Slide 11)
1. **Product Innovation**:
   - Introducing a new or significantly improved good with enhanced technical specifications, components, durability, or utility.
   - Example: Solar-powered cold-storage hubs for rural tomato farmers.
2. **Process Innovation**:
   - Implementing a new or significantly improved production, delivery, or logistics method that increases operational efficiency, lowers unit costs, or reduces waste.
   - Example: Automated inventory barcodes or real-time GPS fleet telemetry replacing paper manifests in haulage.
3. **Service Innovation**:
   - Introducing a new or upgraded customer experience, support system, or interaction protocol.
   - Example: Automated SMS appointment notifications, hospital telemedicine triage, or doorstep laundry returns.
4. **Marketing Innovation**:
   - Implementing a new marketing method involving significant changes in product design, packaging, placement, promotion, or pricing strategies.
   - Example: Direct-to-consumer influencer bundling, micro-sachet packaging (allowing low-income consumers to purchase detergent or milk in tiny daily quantities).
5. **Business Model Innovation**:
   - Fundamentally redesigning the architecture through which an enterprise creates, delivers, and captures value.
   - Example: Shifting from selling expensive diesel generators outright to a "Power-as-a-Service" (pay-per-kilowatt-hour) metered solar subscription model.

---

### 4. Degrees of Novelty & Innovation Taxonomy
- **Incremental Innovation**:
  - Continuous, small-scale refinements, optimizations, and extensions of existing products, technologies, or processes.
  - Low technical and market risk; short development timeframes; accounts for ~85% of commercial innovations (e.g., annual smartphone camera updates, packaging size adjustments).
- **Radical / Breakthrough Innovation**:
  - Discontinuous breakthroughs that introduce entirely novel technological architectures or foundational capabilities, often creating brand-new industries.
  - High risk, massive R&D expenditure, long development horizons (e.g., invention of the transistor, internet protocols, mRNA vaccines).
- **Disruptive Innovation (Clayton Christensen)**:
  - An innovation that enters at the bottom of a market (or creates a new market) with a simpler, cheaper, more convenient solution that initially underperforms established products on traditional metrics.
  - Incumbents ignore it because profit margins at the low end are unattractive.
  - The disruptive entrant steadily improves its quality, eventually migrating upmarket and displacing established market leaders (e.g., Netflix streaming displacing Blockbuster; mobile money agents displacing traditional brick-and-mortar bank branches).

### 5. Sources of Innovation (Peter Drucker's Seven Sources)
1. *The Unexpected*: Unexpected market successes (revealing hidden demand) or unexpected failures (exposing flawed assumptions).
2. *Incongruities*: The gap between reality as it actually exists and reality as people assume it ought to be (e.g., high hospital healthcare costs despite doctor surpluses).
3. *Process Need*: A glaring bottleneck or missing link in an existing workflow (e.g., slow paper-based bank clearing creating the need for electronic interbank switching).
4. *Industry and Market Structure Changes*: Rapid growth, deregulation, or technological shifts that unseat complacent incumbents.
5. *Demographics*: Shifts in population size, age distribution, urbanization rates, or education (e.g., Nigeria's median age of ~18 years driving rapid adoption of digital-first mobile commerce).
6. *Changes in Perception, Mood, and Meaning*: Cultural shifts toward healthy eating, environmental sustainability, or local cultural pride.
7. *New Knowledge*: Breakthrough scientific or technological discoveries (e.g., artificial intelligence, gene editing).

### 6. Schumpeterian Creative Destruction (Deck 4 & Slide 7)
- **Definition**: The incessant process of economic transformation where innovative new technologies, products, and business models continuously replace, obsolete, and destroy outdated, inefficient firms and legacy industries from within.
- **Economic Consequence**: Creative destruction causes short-term friction (bank branches closing, typists displaced by computers), but drives long-term macroeconomic productivity, living standard improvements, and technological advancement.
- **Nigerian Real-World Cases**:
  - *GSM Telecommunications (MTN, Airtel, Glo)*: Wiped out the state-owned NITEL landline monopoly within 3 years, unleashing a multi-billion dollar mobile economy.
  - *Fintech Switches (Interswitch, Paystack, Flutterwave, Moniepoint)*: Obsoleted manual paper cheque clearing and long bank hall queues with instant digital transfers and ubiquitous POS agency banking networks.
