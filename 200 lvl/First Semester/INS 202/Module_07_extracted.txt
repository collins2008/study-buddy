7.2 Why Prototype?
Traditional software development: you can’t test until you implement
Implementation is expensive, and there is nothing to test until you have made that expenditure of effort and schedule time
Result: any design errors are built in to the first thing you can test, and it is very expensive to make changes
Result: design errors, unless they are really bad, are left in the product
=== SLIDE 4 ===
07: Prototyping
Copyright © 2004 by Prentice Hall
Breaking this implementation paradox
Build a prototype of the basic functionality, especially the interface
Test the prototype, which will uncover design errors
Correct the errors
Repeat until you have a clean design
A major tool for improving usability
Heavily used in industry

Goal of prototyping a Web site
The goal of prototyping is to provide a way to observe aspects of the Web site to evaluate design ideas and to consider alternatives before committing to an implementation.

Wireframing
A wireframe is an illustration of a page’s interface that focuses on space allocation, prioritization of the page’s content, available functionalities, and behaviors. 
Wireframes do not include any styling, color, or graphics. 
They can be low-fidelity (i.e., tend to be abstract because they use simple images to block off space and implement mock content)
High-fidelity that have a higher level of detail (i.e., include information about each item, including dimensions, behaviors or actions related to the element.

Horizontal prototype: broad but only top-level
A horizontal prototype has little depth of functionality (or no functionality) but is broad in terms of the number of features it presents. 
Useful for presenting an overview or the “big picture” perspective of a site’s “look.”
=== SLIDE 11 ===

Vertical prototype: deep, but only some functions
A vertical prototype presents only a limited number of features, but the functionality of those features is fully developed
=== SLIDE 12 ===
Global, Local & Isolated Prototypes
A global prototype has breadth and depth. It is a prototype of the entire site. 
A local prototype is used to test design alternatives for a difficult issue. 
It is a way to resolve problems when discussions on a design choice have come to an impasse. 
An isolated prototype is not connected to any other pages in the Web site

Benefits of prototyping
Improves the chances of creating a usable product, since mistakes and omissions are flushed out early in the development process, not after implementation 
Higher user satisfaction
Users are good at criticizing an existing system; they are much less able to imagine how specifications would translate into a product
Prototyping brings the users into the process at a much earlier stage

=== SLIDE 21 ===
Advantages offered by low fidelity prototyping
It is easy to build. 
It is not necessary to wait for the developers to create a computer-based prototype. 
It is fast to change. Erasing one link name and writing in another is far quicker with a pencil than with any web-authoring tool. 
It maximizes the number of times the design is refined before anything is committed to implementation.
07: Prototyping
Copyright © 2004 by Prentice Hall
=== SLIDE 22 ===
Disadvantages – Low fidelity Prototypes
If your low fidelity prototype is made from paper, you may need to create some convention for indicating that an item is “clickable,” since users will not be able to watch the cursor change as it passes over a link.  
They do not “show” well to management, since they do have a polished look to them. 
They do not simulate response times accurately.

=== SLIDE 40 ===
Problems that can occur with high fidelity prototypes
Correctly functioning software takes too long to build. By the time there is anything that can be used for testing the design, much of the project’s development budget has already been spent. 
Changes take too long. 
One bug can destroy a user test. 
It can create unrealistic expectations in management.
