FOUNDATIONS
1. Introduction to Opportunity Identification
ENTREPRENEURSHIP AND INNOVATION  |  OPPORTUNITY IDENTIFICATION & EVALUATION
3
Entrepreneurship begins with recognising a need, problem or possibility that can be turned into value.
Opportunity identification is the process of discovering or recognising potential business opportunities.
Opportunity evaluation examines whether an identified possibility is practical, commercially attractive and worth pursuing.
A business idea is not automatically an opportunity: it must be connected to real demand and a workable solution.
Example: complaints about expensive, inconvenient campus meals may suggest a food-delivery opportunity—but demand, costs and delivery arrangements must first be investigated.
=== SLIDE 4 ===
FOUNDATIONS
Business Idea versus Business Opportunity
ENTREPRENEURSHIP AND INNOVATION  |  OPPORTUNITY IDENTIFICATION & EVALUATION
4
BUSINESS IDEA
A thought, concept or proposed product or service.

Example: “I want to start a laundry business.”
BUSINESS OPPORTUNITY
An idea supported by evidence of customer need, a reachable market and a realistic way to create value.

Example: research shows students need reliable same-day laundry collection and delivery and are willing to pay for it.
An idea suggests what could be done; an opportunity provides a reason to believe it can work.

=== SLIDE 10 ===
UNDERSTANDING THE BUSINESS ENVIRONMENT
3. Environmental Scanning
ENTREPRENEURSHIP AND INNOVATION  |  OPPORTUNITY IDENTIFICATION & EVALUATION
10
Environmental scanning is the systematic observation and analysis of factors that may create opportunities or threats for a business.
It helps entrepreneurs understand changes in customers, competitors, technology, economic conditions and regulation.
Scanning is more than noticing a trend: the entrepreneur considers how the change may affect demand, costs, operations and business prospects.
Example: unreliable electricity combined with rising interest in solar power may indicate demand for solar installation and maintenance.

INTERNAL ENVIRONMENT

• Human resources: skills and experience.
• Financial resources: capital and cash flow.
• Physical resources: land, buildings and equipment.
• Organisational culture and management.
• Technology, systems and capabilities.
EXTERNAL ENVIRONMENT
Factors outside the direct control of the business:

• Political and regulatory conditions.
• Economic conditions and purchasing power.
• Social and demographic trends.
• Technological developments.
• Environmental conditions and competition.

=== SLIDE 14 ===
FINDING DEMAND
4. Identifying Unmet Customer Needs
ENTREPRENEURSHIP AND INNOVATION  |  OPPORTUNITY IDENTIFICATION & EVALUATION
14
An unmet need exists when a customer requirement is unavailable, inaccessible, inadequate or poorly satisfied.
Unserved needs: no suitable product or service is available.
Underserved needs: an offering exists, but its quantity, quality, affordability or accessibility is inadequate.
Poorly satisfied needs: products exist, but customers are dissatisfied with their quality or delivery.
Emerging needs: new requirements arise from changes in technology, lifestyles or the wider environment.
=== SLIDE 15 ===
FINDING DEMAND
How to Discover Unmet Needs
ENTREPRENEURSHIP AND INNOVATION  |  OPPORTUNITY IDENTIFICATION & EVALUATION
15
LISTEN
Conduct interviews and ask customers about difficulties, frustrations and desired improvements.
OBSERVE
Watch how people use products and services. Notice delays, workarounds and repeated problems.
INVESTIGATE
Use surveys, complaints, online reviews, community engagement and competitor analysis.
Do not assume a problem is widespread: gather evidence from potential customers.
=== SLIDE 16 ===
FINDING DEMAND
Example: Unmet Need in a University Community
ENTREPRENEURSHIP AND INNOVATION  |  OPPORTUNITY IDENTIFICATION & EVALUATION
16
Observation: students struggle to obtain affordable meals after 6 p.m.
Initial evidence: many food vendors close early; students face queues and some live far from food outlets.
Possible solution: a low-cost meal ordering and delivery service using WhatsApp and designated collection points.
Questions to investigate: how many students need the service, what price can they pay, and how frequently will they order?
Also investigate food safety, vendor partnerships, delivery times and the cost of fulfilling each order.
=== SLIDE 17 ===
FINDING DEMAND
5. Identifying Market Gaps
ENTREPRENEURSHIP AND INNOVATION  |  OPPORTUNITY IDENTIFICATION & EVALUATION
17
A market gap is an area of customer demand that is not adequately served by existing businesses or available products and services.
Price gap: existing options are too expensive for a particular customer group.
Quality gap: products or services fail to meet expected standards.
Accessibility gap: customers face distance, inconvenient hours or poor distribution.
Product or service gap: customers require an offering that is not currently available.
Customer-segment gap: existing providers overlook a particular group of potential customers.

CREATING VALUE FROM WHAT IS AVAILABLE
6. Resource Utilisation
ENTREPRENEURSHIP AND INNOVATION  |  OPPORTUNITY IDENTIFICATION & EVALUATION
19
Resource utilisation means using available resources effectively and efficiently to produce goods, deliver services or create value.
Resources include human skills, finance, land, buildings, machinery, raw materials, technology and information.
Idle assets can be redeployed: an unused building may become a co-working or training centre.
Waste can become an input: agricultural by-products may be processed into animal feed or organic fertiliser.
Skills and knowledge can become services: professional expertise may support a consultancy or training business.
Shared-resource models can give customers access to facilities they cannot afford to own individually.

HIGHER PRODUCTIVITY

Entrepreneurial question: What resources are already available, and how can they be used more productively?
=== SLIDE 21 ===
TESTING THE OPPORTUNITY
7. Preliminary Feasibility Assessment
ENTREPRENEURSHIP AND INNOVATION  |  OPPORTUNITY IDENTIFICATION & EVALUATION
21
Preliminary feasibility assessment is an initial investigation to determine whether a proposed business idea is practical and has enough potential to justify further study.
It helps identify major challenges early, reduce uncertainty and avoid committing resources to weak ideas.
It is an early screening exercise—not a substitute for a detailed feasibility study or business plan.
The entrepreneur considers whether customers will buy, whether the solution can be delivered, whether resources are available and whether the business can operate lawfully and sustainably.
=== SLIDE 22 ===
TESTING THE OPPORTUNITY
Feasibility: Market and Technical
ENTREPRENEURSHIP AND INNOVATION  |  OPPORTUNITY IDENTIFICATION & EVALUATION
22
MARKET FEASIBILITY
Examines whether sufficient demand exists.

Ask:
• Who are the customers?
• What problem is being solved?
• How large is the potential market?
• What are customers willing and able to pay?
• Who are the competitors?
• Is demand growing, stable or declining?
TECHNICAL FEASIBILITY
Examines whether the product or service can be produced or delivered.

Ask:
• What equipment and technology are required?
• Are skilled people available?
• Is the location suitable?
• Are power, water and internet available?
• Can required quality be maintained?
=== SLIDE 23 ===
TESTING THE OPPORTUNITY
Feasibility: Financial and Operational
ENTREPRENEURSHIP AND INNOVATION  |  OPPORTUNITY IDENTIFICATION & EVALUATION
23
FINANCIAL FEASIBILITY
Examines whether the business can generate acceptable returns.

Consider:
• Start-up and working capital.
• Operating costs and expected revenue.
• Pricing and profit margins.
• Cash-flow needs and financing sources.
• Financial risks and likely returns.
OPERATIONAL FEASIBILITY
Examines whether day-to-day activities can be performed effectively.

Consider:
• Employees and responsibilities.
• Supply of materials.
• Production and service processes.
• Inventory and distribution.
• Customer service and quality control.
=== SLIDE 24 ===
TESTING THE OPPORTUNITY
Feasibility: Legal and Management
ENTREPRENEURSHIP AND INNOVATION  |  OPPORTUNITY IDENTIFICATION & EVALUATION
24
LEGAL FEASIBILITY
Examines whether the business can operate within applicable laws and regulations.

Consider:
• Business registration.
• Licences and permits.
• Tax obligations.
• Labour and safety rules.
• Environmental and consumer protection requirements.
MANAGEMENT FEASIBILITY
Examines whether the entrepreneur and team can manage the business.

Consider:
• Industry knowledge and experience.
• Financial and managerial skills.
• Leadership and decision-making.
• Ability to organise people and resources.
• Capacity to manage risks.

=== SLIDE 27 ===
APPLICATION
Case Study: Campus Meal Delivery
ENTREPRENEURSHIP AND INNOVATION  |  OPPORTUNITY IDENTIFICATION & EVALUATION
27
Situation: students struggle to obtain affordable meals after 6 p.m.; vendors close early, queues are long and some students live far from food outlets.
Proposed idea: a meal-ordering and delivery service using WhatsApp and designated collection points.
Opportunity evidence to seek: student demand, willingness to pay, order frequency and dissatisfaction with existing options.
Environmental issues: student population, purchasing power, competitors, smartphone access and food-safety requirements.
Resources: partner food vendors, smartphones, student delivery workers and a low-cost ordering channel.
Feasibility questions: can orders be delivered on time, safely and at a price that covers costs?
=== SLIDE 28 ===
APPLICATION
Case Study: What Should the Students Do?
ENTREPRENEURSHIP AND INNOVATION  |  OPPORTUNITY IDENTIFICATION & EVALUATION
28
TEST DEMAND
Interview students and run a short survey. Record preferred meals, delivery times, order frequency and willingness to pay.
TEST OPERATIONS
Speak with vendors. Map delivery routes, estimate delivery time and define safe food-handling procedures.
TEST ECONOMICS
Estimate meal costs, packaging, delivery and other expenses. Check whether the proposed price can cover costs.
A small pilot can provide evidence before the students commit substantial resources.
=== SLIDE 29 ===
CRITICAL CONSIDERATIONS
9. Challenges in Opportunity Identification and Evaluation
ENTREPRENEURSHIP AND INNOVATION  |  OPPORTUNITY IDENTIFICATION & EVALUATION
29
Inadequate market information: decisions may be based on assumptions rather than reliable evidence.
Limited finance: a promising idea may require more capital than the entrepreneur can access.
Competition: established businesses may have stronger brands, customer relationships and economies of scale.
Uncertainty: prices, policies, technology and customer preferences can change.
Poor feasibility assessment: revenue may be overestimated while costs and regulatory requirements are underestimated.
Inadequate entrepreneurial skills and fear of failure may prevent effective execution.
