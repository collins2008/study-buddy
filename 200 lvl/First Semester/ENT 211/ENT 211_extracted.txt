Innovation is not just having an idea. It involves putting that idea into practice so that customers, employees or communities benefit. It can occur in healthcare, education, retail, agriculture and public services—not only in technology companies.
=== SLIDE 10 ===
09 / MAKE THE DISTINCTION
Invention vs. innovation
ENTREPRENEURSHIP & INNOVATION  •  LECTURE NOTES
10
INVENTION
Creating or discovering something new.Example: developing a prototype of a low-cost medical device.
INNOVATION
Implementing an idea in a way that creates practical value.Example: producing, distributing and using the device reliably in clinics.
An invention may never be adopted. Innovation requires application and value; it does not always require a completely new invention.

Practical innovation strategies

2
Ask

3

=== SLIDE 17 ===
16 / APPLY THE CONCEPTS
Mini case: improving a local pharmacy
ENTREPRENEURSHIP & INNOVATION  •  LECTURE NOTES
17
A neighbourhood pharmacy often runs out of common medicines while other products remain on the shelf for months.
1
Opportunity
Stock-outs frustrate customers and can mean lost sales.
2
Possible innovation
Use simple digital sales and inventory records to identify fast-moving products.
3
Test & measure
Pilot the system and track stock-outs, expired stock and customer satisfaction.
Discuss: What information should the owner collect first? What could go wrong? How would you know whether the innovation created value?
